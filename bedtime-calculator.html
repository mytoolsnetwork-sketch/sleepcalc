What Time Should I Go to Sleep?

The perfect bedtime depends on when you need to wake up and how many complete 90-minute sleep cycles you want to get. Most adults need 5 to 6 cycles per night — that's 7.5 to 9 hours of sleep. Going to bed at the right time means waking up at the end of a cycle, feeling refreshed instead of groggy.

Bedtimes Based on Common Wake-Up Times

If you need to wake up at 6:00 AM

The ideal bedtimes are 8:46 PM (6 cycles), 10:16 PM (5 cycles), or 11:46 PM (4 cycles). Most adults do best going to bed at 10:16 PM.

If you need to wake up at 7:00 AM

The ideal bedtimes are 9:46 PM (6 cycles), 11:16 PM (5 cycles), or 12:46 AM (4 cycles).

If you need to wake up at 8:00 AM

The ideal bedtimes are 10:46 PM (6 cycles), 12:16 AM (5 cycles), or 1:46 AM (4 cycles).

Tips for Sticking to Your Bedtime

  • Set a bedtime alarm — not just a wake-up alarm. A reminder 30 minutes before bedtime helps you wind down.
  • Avoid screens 1 hour before bed — blue light delays melatonin production and pushes your natural sleep time later.
  • Keep weekends consistent — sleeping in more than 1 hour on weekends causes "social jet lag" that disrupts your whole week.
  • Create a wind-down routine — reading, stretching, or a warm shower signals to your brain that sleep is coming.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time should I go to bed if I wake up at 6 AM?

To wake up at 6:00 AM feeling refreshed, go to bed at 8:46 PM (9 hours, 6 cycles) or 10:16 PM (7.5 hours, 5 cycles). The 10:16 PM bedtime is the most realistic for most adults.

Is it bad to go to bed after midnight?

Not necessarily — what matters most is getting enough complete sleep cycles and being consistent. However, going to bed very late often cuts into morning sleep cycles, which are the most REM-rich and important for memory and mood.

How do I fall asleep faster at my new bedtime?

Start shifting your bedtime by 15 minutes every few days rather than jumping straight to the new time. Your circadian rhythm adjusts gradually — forcing a sudden change often leads to lying awake frustrated.

Does this calculator work for shift workers?

Yes — just enter whatever your next wake-up time is, regardless of whether it's AM or PM. Sleep cycles work the same way no matter when you sleep.
