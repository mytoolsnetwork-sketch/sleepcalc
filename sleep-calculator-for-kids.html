What Time Should Kids Go to Bed?

Children need significantly more sleep than adults. The exact bedtime depends on your child's age and what time they need to wake up — for school, activities, or family routine. Our sleep calculator for kids takes both into account and gives you a precise recommended bedtime.

Why Sleep Is So Important for Children

  • Brain development — sleep is when the brain consolidates learning and forms new neural connections.
  • Physical growth — growth hormone is released primarily during deep sleep.
  • Emotional regulation — sleep-deprived children are more prone to tantrums, anxiety, and difficulty managing emotions.
  • Immune system — adequate sleep strengthens the immune response in children.
  • Attention and behavior — sleep-deprived children often appear hyperactive rather than tired, which can be mistaken for ADHD.

Tips for Getting Kids to Bed on Time

  • Start the routine early — begin winding down 45–60 minutes before lights out.
  • No screens before bed — blue light suppresses melatonin in children even more than adults.
  • Keep it consistent — the same bedtime every night (including weekends) regulates the body clock.
  • Make the room dark and cool — children sleep better in a slightly cool, dark room.
  • Use a calming routine — bath, story, soft music — predictable steps signal that sleep is coming.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time should a 5-year-old go to bed?

A 5-year-old who wakes up at 7:00 AM should go to bed between 6:45 PM and 8:00 PM to get the recommended 11–13 hours of sleep. Most kindergarten-aged children do best with a 7:00–7:30 PM bedtime.

What time should a 10-year-old go to bed?

A 10-year-old who wakes up at 7:00 AM for school should go to bed between 8:45 PM and 9:15 PM to get 9–10 hours of sleep.

What time should a 13-year-old go to bed?

A 13-year-old who wakes up at 7:00 AM needs 8–9 hours of sleep, so bedtime should be between 9:45 PM and 10:45 PM. Teenagers' circadian rhythms naturally shift later, making early bedtimes increasingly difficult.

How do I know if my child is getting enough sleep?

A well-rested child wakes up naturally or with minimal prompting, is alert and in a good mood during the day, and doesn't fall asleep in the car or during quiet activities. Consistent difficulty waking up is a clear sign of insufficient sleep.
